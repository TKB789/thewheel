      Covered calls only, week by week

      A call every Monday on the same 100 shares, even after an assigned week, to show how often each strike method gets assigned. The full wheel, with puts, is in the section above.

      How the recommendations would have done

      Every Monday and Thursday the update job logs the call your routine would sell: the schedule pick for Monday → Wednesday, and on Thursday whichever of Friday or Monday pays more per day. Weeks where it said to hold off are logged too, as if you'd sold anyway, so you can see whether holding off helped. Results use the closing price on expiration day: premium kept, minus $0.65 per contract, minus the upside you'd have given up if the shares were called away. For puts, the cost is paying more than the market price for the shares.

      By strategy

      Each way of picking a strike, tracked side by side. The alternatives weren't counted in the totals above, since you can only sell one call on 100 shares.

      Trades

      How it decides. Calls are recommended near 0.20 delta (0.15 when the stock is running hot), the common range for covered calls when you want to keep your shares. Cash-secured puts target 0.25 delta (0.15 when the stock is sliding), the usual range when you'd be glad to own the shares. Expirations that span an earnings report are skipped. "Chance of assignment" is the market-implied probability the option finishes in the money, from Black-Scholes using each option's own implied volatility.

      Data comes from Yahoo Finance through a scheduled job in this repository, delayed about 15 minutes and only as fresh as the last run. Confirm the quote in your brokerage before trading. This is a decision aid, not financial advice.
