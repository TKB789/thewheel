Wheel Analyzer

Goal: keep the shares long term. If they get called away, sell cash-secured puts to buy back in (the wheel).

    How the wheel works, and what this site does
    1 · Own 100 shares: sell a

    You're paid a now for agreeing to sell your shares at a set price, the , if the stock closes above it on the date. Below it, you keep the shares and the premium, and sell another.

    2 · Shares : sell a

    With the cash from the sale, you're paid a premium for agreeing to buy 100 shares back at a strike below today's price. If the stock stays above it, you keep the cash and the premium, and sell another.

    3 · Put : you own shares again

    You bought back at a price you chose, lowered by every premium collected along the way. Back to step 1.

    The trade-off. Premiums are steady income, but they cap the upside: if the stock jumps past your call strike you sell too early, and if it keeps falling after a put is assigned you've bought on the way down. The wheel tends to do best on stocks that drift sideways and worst on ones that climb fast. The Backtest tab shows which kind your stock has been.

    How strikes are picked. By , which is close to the market's own odds that the option ends up exercised: about 0.20 for calls (roughly a 1-in-5 chance your shares are called away) and 0.25 for puts. Warnings (earnings in the window, a stock , thin premium) move the strike further out or say to hold off.

    The tabs. Covered calls is step 1 and Cash-secured puts is step 2, each with today's pick and your weekly schedule. Paper trades tracks the trades you choose. Backtest replays the whole wheel on 10 years of prices. Research tests whether anything predicts assignment better than delta.

    Words with a dotted underline can be tapped for a short definition. Data comes from Yahoo Finance a few times a day, about 15 minutes delayed; confirm prices in your brokerage before trading.

        Your paper trades

        Keep separate portfolios to try different strategies side by side: your usual routine in one, a closer strike or one-day trades in another. Log a trade with "Paper trade this" on a card, or "Try" on any row of an option chain, and choose its portfolio. Each is scored after its expiration day's close: the premium you entered, minus the $0.65 fee, minus any upside given up (calls) or loss below the strike (puts). How the site's own picks would have done over the years is in the Backtest tab.

        How it decides. Calls are recommended near 0.20 delta (0.15 when the stock is running hot), the common range for covered calls when you want to keep your shares. Cash-secured puts target 0.25 delta (0.15 when the stock is sliding), the usual range when you'd be glad to own the shares. Expirations that span an earnings report are skipped. "Chance of assignment" is the market-implied probability the option finishes in the money, from Black-Scholes using each option's own implied volatility.

        Data comes from Yahoo Finance through a scheduled job in this repository, delayed about 15 minutes and only as fresh as the last run. Confirm the quote in your brokerage before trading. This is a decision aid, not financial advice.
